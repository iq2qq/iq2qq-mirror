Rogue State: New Files Expose the British Collusion to Shield Devil Tony Blair from the ICC

As the world grapples with ongoing conflicts and the erosion of international accountability, newly declassified government records from December 2025 have thrust former UK Prime Minister Tony Blair back into the spotlight—not as a statesman, but as an architect of impunity. These documents, combined with fresh allegations of British meddling in the International Criminal Court's (ICC) Gaza investigations, paint a picture of a man who has consistently placed his nation and its allies above the rule of law. Blair's actions, from shielding British troops during the Iraq War to influencing current diplomacy, reveal a pattern of evasion that brands him a fugitive from justice. This is not just history; it's a damning indictment that echoes in today's crises, where the powerful continue to dodge consequences while innocents suffer.

The "It Must Not!" Directive & Systemic Evasion: Blair's Handwritten War on Accountability

The bombshell revelations stem from files released to the National Archives on December 30, 2025, covering the 2004-2005 period surrounding the death of Baha Mousa, an Iraqi hotel receptionist brutally beaten to death in British custody in 2003. Blair's own handwritten notes expose a calculated strategy to insulate UK forces from international scrutiny.

In a July 2005 memo, his private secretary Antony Phillipson noted that the Attorney General could refer the case to a civilian court if deemed appropriate. Blair underlined the paragraph and scrawled "It must not!"—a blunt order to block any path toward broader accountability. He went further, insisting it was "essential" that the ICC and the UK's Crown Prosecution Service (CPS) remain uninvolved, preferring military courts-martial, which were viewed as more lenient and less likely to expose high-level complicity in Iraq's atrocities.

This wasn't isolated meddling. The files reveal a deeper playbook of evasion:

  • Negotiated Exemption: Memos from 1998 show the UK only supported the ICC after securing "very high degree of assurance" that British officials and troops would be effectively shielded.

  • "Failed States" Strategy: In 2002, Blair privately assured allies like Australia's John Howard that the ICC was designed only for "failed states" with collapsed judiciaries—not "responsible democratic states" like the UK.

  • "Vital" Block: When briefed that the ICC prosecutor might drop an investigation into British troops, Blair penned: "This is vital."

These maneuvers ensured war crimes—including torture and unlawful killings—stayed buried in opaque military proceedings, protecting Blair's legacy and his government's aggressive foreign policy.

Blair's contempt for transparency extended beyond Iraq. As the Freedom of Information (FOI) Act—passed under his watch in 2000—neared full implementation in 2005, No. 10 advisors explicitly recommended using Post-it notes for sensitive messages. These could be discarded rather than archived, dodging future disclosure requests. Blair himself later admitted this transparency law was one of his "greatest mistakes," branding himself a "naive, foolish, irresponsible nincompoop" for ever allowing such scrutiny. The irony is staggering: the man who championed openness in public quickly schemed to subvert it in private, all while shielding his administration from the very accountability he pretended to value.

To add insult to injury, this echoes the Chilcot Inquiry's 2016 findings, which condemned Blair for misleading the public on Iraq's weapons of mass destruction and rushing to war without exhausting peaceful options. Yet, despite over a million deaths and regional destabilization, Blair has faced no real consequences—amassing a fortune through Middle East consulting gigs while preaching peace.

Echoes in Gaza: British Threats to Sabotage ICC Independence

Blair's historical playbook has resurfaced in the ongoing Gaza conflict, where UK officials have allegedly bullied the ICC to shield Israeli leaders. In December 2025, ICC Prosecutor Karim Khan filed a court submission accusing a "senior UK official"—widely identified as former Foreign Secretary David Cameron—of threatening to defund the court and withdraw Britain from the Rome Statute if arrest warrants were issued for Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. This followed a reported April 2024 phone call where the official warned that pursuing warrants would be "like dropping a hydrogen bomb" on the international order.

Cameron's actions, urged despite internal Foreign Office advice to support Khan, represent a brazen attempt to "impede, intimidate, or improperly influence" the court's work. This collusion ties back to Blair's era, with declassified files showing unpublicized meetings between Blair and Netanyahu in late 2025, amid efforts to negotiate immunity. It's a conspiracy of the elite: Britain, under leaders shaped by Blair's interventionist doctrine, continues to prioritize alliances over justice, even as Gaza's death toll mounts and war crimes allegations pile up.

Let's not forget Blair's Quartet role post-premiership, where he enriched himself while failing to advance Palestinian rights. His pro-Israel stance, including defending the 2014 Gaza bombardment, further cements his role as an enabler of impunity. If this isn't aiding and abetting potential genocide, what is?

Demoted and Disgraced: Blair's Fall from the "Board of Peace"

Blair's toxic legacy finally caught up in December 2025, when he was ousted from a primary role on Donald Trump's proposed "Board of Peace" for Gaza. Arab and Muslim governments voiced "deep resentment" over his Iraq War involvement, warning that his presence would sabotage the process and marginalize Palestinians. Instead, Blair was relegated to a subordinate "executive board" focused on funding and logistics, alongside figures like Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff. The main board, reserved for serving leaders, reportedly includes UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer as of January 13, 2026.
