AI Overview: Cultural Nationalism:

"...It focuses on cultivating a nation's identity, history, and destiny, often through the work of intellectuals and artists. While distinct from political nationalism, which seeks national self-determination through statehood, cultural nationalism can contribute to and inform political movements. 

Here's a more detailed look:

  • Focus on Culture: Cultural nationalism prioritizes a shared sense of national belonging rooted in traditions, customs, language, and values. 

  • Moral Community: Unlike political nationalism's focus on political autonomy, cultural nationalism emphasizes the cultivation of a moral community based on shared cultural values. 

  • Intellectual and Artistic Role: Intellectuals and artists play a crucial role in shaping and disseminating a vision of the nation's identity, history, and destiny. 

  • Early Phase of National Movements: Cultural nationalism often emerges in the early stages of national movements, sometimes even before explicit political demands for statehood. 

  • Distinct from Ethnic Nationalism: Cultural nationalism is distinct from ethnic nationalism, which relies on shared racial or ethnic characteristics to define national identity. 

  • Interrelation with Political Nationalism: While distinct, cultural nationalism can inform and contribute to political nationalism, as demonstrated by cultural nationalists who may also engage in political activities or see independent statehood as necessary for the defense of national identity."

Britannica: History of Europe:

“...cultural nationalism. The phrase denotes the belief that each nation in Europe had from its earliest formation developed a culture of its own, with features as unique as its language, even though its language and culture might have near relatives over the frontier. Europe was thus seen as a bouquet of diverse flowers harmoniously bunched, rather than as a uniform upper-class civilization stretching from Paris to St. Petersburg, from London to Rome, and from Berlin to Lisbon—wherever “polite society” could be found, a society acknowledging the same artistic ideals… In other words, the revolutionary idea of the people as the source of power ended the idea of a cosmopolitan Europe.”
