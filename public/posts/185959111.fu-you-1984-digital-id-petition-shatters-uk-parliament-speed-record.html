On August 22, 2025, Maxim Sutcliff launched a petition on the UK Parliament petitions platform, demanding the government halt any plans for digital ID cards. Titled "Do not introduce Digital ID cards," it argues that such a system risks mass surveillance and forced state-controlled registration, echoing concerns that led to the scrapping of physical ID cards in 2010.

On September 26, 2025, mere hours after PM Keir Starmer unveiled plans for mandatory digital IDs, the petition exploded—hitting 100,000 signatures in ~24 hours and 1 million in a day, shattering the UK's speed record for parliamentary petitions (beating the 2019 Brexit revocation's 4-day mark). As of September 27, it's surged past 2 million signatures.

This explosive growth reflects widespread opposition to perceived "1984-style" digital tyranny, making it a historic moment in UK civic engagement.
